Lesson 0001

How device memory and email work for local-only apps

Tied to your mission: kid-friendly app, data stays on device, parents get daily/weekly/monthly sheets.

Two meanings of “memory”

In app conversations, memory usually means storage — where your app keeps data on the phone — not RAM. For a kid-friendly app with no cloud account, everything lives in the app’s sandbox: a private folder only your app can open.

As a web developer, think of the sandbox as a hard wall around localStorage and IndexedDB that no other website (or app) can read.

Data in SQLite/AsyncStorage stays on the device until the user deletes the app. You do not need a server for this.

What “send a sheet by email” actually means

Your app does not send email like a backend with SMTP. On iOS and Android, the flow is:

① Read local data (e.g. last 7 days from SQLite) ↓ ② Render HTML summary of the sheet ↓ ③ Print.printToFileAsync → PDF file on device ↓ ④ Sharing.shareAsync(pdfUri) → native share sheet ↓ ⑤ User (or parent) picks Mail → Mail app opens with PDF attached ↓ ⑥ Parent taps Send in their Mail app

The email travels through the parent’s mail account (Gmail, iCloud, etc.). Your app never sees their password or runs a mail server. That is why this pattern fits local-only, kid-safe apps: you are exporting a file, not silently transmitting child data to your backend.

Kids Category note: Apple expects a parental gate before actions that leave the app (email, external links, purchases). A simple math question or “press and hold for 3 seconds” before opening the share sheet is a common pattern. See Apple’s kids design guidance.

Daily, weekly, monthly — what you can automate

You can schedule local notifications (“Ready to send your weekly sheet?”). You cannot reliably silently email on a schedule without user action — iOS and Android block background email for privacy.

Practical pattern for kid apps:

  • Daily/weekly/monthly: notification fires → kid or parent opens app → parental gate → Share sheet → Mail.
  • Optional: remember last-sent date in AsyncStorage so you do not nag twice the same day.

If you later want automatic email with zero taps, you need a server, parental consent, and COPPA-compliant data handling — a much heavier path. For your mission, the share-sheet approach is the right default.

Mental model (one sentence)

Memory = your app’s private SQLite/files on the phone. Email = generate a PDF locally, then hand it to the OS share sheet so the parent’s Mail app sends it.
